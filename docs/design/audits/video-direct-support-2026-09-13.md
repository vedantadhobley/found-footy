# Assigned versus direct source support — September 13, 2026

## Outcome

**Subsequent decision:** the user adopted direct support after this experiment.
The [implementation decision](../../decisions/2026-09-13-popularity-counts-direct-support.md)
records the local runtime policy and verification. The results below preserve
the original assigned baseline and its conditional historical evidence limits.

The user requested an offline comparison of exclusive assigned popularity and
non-exclusive direct support. The comparison leaves quality, keeper selection,
dHash thresholds and public ranking/filter rules unchanged. It evaluates the
recorded selected sets, then separately evaluates the local restoration
planner's projected sets. No production access, writes, rollout or new scoring
policy was authorized or performed.

The methods give identical scores in 276 of the 278 attributable events under
the conditional category assumptions below. After the restoration projection,
direct support changes two events' scores and makes one additional clip visible.
No strict order reversal or unresolved order change occurs in that comparison.
This is evidence about score semantics, not proof of better video selection.

The aggregate export does not prove per-source IDs, outcomes or current credits.

The definitions are deliberately different:

- **Assigned support:** count sources credited to this selected representative.
  Each accepted source has one owner; selected counts conserve the source total.
- **Direct support:** for this clip, sum exact-source observations over its own
  MD5 and every directly matching accepted variant. Each source contributes once
  within the score, even when both dHash routes match. A source may support two
  selected clips, so scores are not additive across the event.

Direct matching retains 12/30/3 or 16/50/5 and event/verification/hash-version
scope. No hidden intermediary supplies transitive evidence. Exact support is
not added again after the MD5 has already contributed. Other selected variants
can also supply direct evidence. Quality/ownership changes alone cannot change
a fixed clip's score when its evidence pool remains fixed.

## Missing validation is a separate variable

156 admitted events have own acceptance for every source variant. The other
122 contain 993 source observations on never-public variants without retained
own validation. The report therefore provides two calculations:

1. **Known-only:** include own accepted active/superseded share categories.
2. **Conditional:** additionally include never-public observations **if** their
   missing own validation confirms the category inherited from their old root.

Removed acceptance is excluded. Object reclamation alone does not erase retained
source evidence; counting a past observation does not require playing its bytes.
That differs intentionally from restoration eligibility. The qualified cohort
has no sources excluded for removed/invalid acceptance.

Dropping all unknown observations changes known-only scores in 122 events,
visibility in five and strict ordering in one against recorded sets. Those
differences mix missing evidence with scoring semantics and are **not** evidence
that direct support itself would cause those regressions. The main comparison
uses the conditional calculation to retain the same recorded evidence population.
The all-own-acceptance cohort has no scoring differences.

## Interpretation and remaining choice

The graph is not creating an arithmetic double count in assigned popularity.
The two approaches answer different questions. Direct support removes the
exclusive-owner choice from a clip's score and avoids credit through nonmatching
lineage. Assigned support preserves a partition of source observations, but its
distribution depends on representation and ownership policy.

Neither metric measures image quality, trustworthy authors, or certainty that
the video depicts the right goal. Reposts remain separate source observations.
Direct dHash overlap may be only a short common section: a compilation or weak
match could raise several clips' scores. The historical comparison exposes a
cleaner possible support meaning; it does not validate every match as substantive
corroboration or show a broad quality improvement.

Keep exact observation identity, graph evidence and keeper quality separate.
Non-exclusive direct support is an explicitly requested experiment, **not** a
replacement for the recorded one-owner runtime contract. No threshold exemption,
new quality model, fractional credit, schema field or API contract was adopted.
Before changing public scoring, settle whether existing direct-match evidence
is sufficient support for visibility, particularly for the newly exposed clip.
